## Blueprint sections in detail

- **§1 The Definition of Riemann Surfaces** — book 1-9, PDF 7-15. Definitions of complex charts/atlases (1.1), complex structures (1.3), Riemann surfaces (1.4), with the examples C, P^1, and tori C/Γ (1.5); no substantial theorems, all foundational definitions.
- **§2 Elementary Properties of Holomorphic Mappings** — book 10-13, PDF 16-19. Thm 2.1 gives the local normal form z ↦ z^k of a holomorphic map, yielding openness (2.4), injective ⇒ biholomorphic (2.5), the Maximum Principle (2.6), compactness ⇒ surjectivity (2.7), constancy of holomorphic functions on compact surfaces (2.8), rationality of meromorphic functions on P^1 (2.9), Liouville (2.10), and the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra (2.11).
- **§4 Branched and Unbranched Coverings** — book 20-31, PDF 26-37. Non-constant holomorphic maps are open and discrete (Thm 4.2), unbranched iff local homeomorphism (Thm 4.4), unique lifted complex structure through a local homeomorphism (Thm 4.6), uniqueness of lifting (Thm 4.8), curve-lifting characterization of coverings (Thm 4.19), proper local homeomorphisms are coverings (Thm 4.22), and a proper non-constant holomorphic map takes every value exactly n times counting multiplicities (Thm 4.24, with Cor 4.25: zeros = poles).
- **§5 The Universal Covering and Covering Transformations** — book 31-40, PDF 37-46. Simply connected coverings are universal (Thm 5.2), the universal covering exists (Thm 5.3, book 32-33 / PDF 38-39), followed by deck transformations Deck(Y/X) and Galois coverings (5.4-5.6) with the identification of Deck with the fundamental group.
- **§6 Sheaves** — book 40-44, PDF 46-50. Definitions of presheaves (6.1), the sheaf axioms (6.3), stalks and germs via inductive limits (6.5), and the associated étale space |F| with its topology; a definitional section with no major theorems.
- **§8 Algebraic Functions** — book 48-59, PDF 54-65. Elementary symmetric functions of a covering extend across branch points (Thm 8.2), coverings extend uniquely across critical values (Thm 8.4, 8.5), and the main Thm 8.9 (book 53-54 / PDF 59-60) constructs, uniquely, the Riemann surface Y → X with F ∈ M(Y) solving an irreducible polynomial P(T) ∈ M(X)[T], ending with Puiseux series (book 58-59).
- **§9 Differential Forms** — book 59-68, PDF 65-74. Defines the cotangent space T_a^(1) = m_a/m_a^2 (9.3) with basis theorem 9.4 (d_a x, d_a y and d_a z, d_a z̄ are bases), the sheaves E^(1), E^(1,0), E^(0,1), Ω, M^(1) of 1- and 2-forms, exterior differentiation d, d', d'', residues, and pullback F* of forms.
- **§10 The Integration of Differential Forms** — book 68-81, PDF 74-87. Line integrals of 1-forms (10.1) with ∫_c dF = F(end) − F(start) (Thm 10.2), **10.4 Local Existence of Primitives of closed forms on disks (book 70-71 / PDF 76-77)**, **Thm 10.5: every closed 1-form has a primitive on some covering X̃ → X (book 71 / PDF 77)**, period homomorphisms, integration of 2-forms, Stokes' Theorem (10.19), ∫∫ dω = 0 for compactly supported ω (Thm 10.20), and **the Residue Theorem: Thm 10.21, Σ Res_{a_k}(ω) = 0 on a compact surface (book 80 / PDF 86)** with Cor 10.22 (zeros = poles).
- **§12 Cohomology Groups** — book 96-104, PDF 102-110. Defines Čech cochains, cocycles, H^1(U,F) and H^1(X,F) as a direct limit (12.1-12.5); H^1(X,E) = 0 for the sheaf of smooth functions via partitions of unity (Thm 12.6), H^1(X,C) = H^1(X,Z) = 0 for simply connected X (Thm 12.7), and Leray's Theorem H^1(X,F) ≅ H^1(U,F) for coverings acyclic for F (Thm 12.8).
- **§13 Dolbeault's Lemma** — book 104-109, PDF 110-115. Solves ∂f/∂z̄ = g first for g of compact support (Lemma 13.1), then **Thm 13.2 (book 105 / PDF 111): on any disk X = {|z| < R ≤ ∞} and any g ∈ E(X) there is f with ∂f/∂z̄ = g**, giving H^1(X,O) = 0 for the disk (Thm 13.4).
- **§14 A Finiteness Theorem** — book 109-118, PDF 115-124. Using L^2-norms on holomorphic functions (14.1-14.3) proves the key finiteness theorem 14.9: dim Im(H^1(X,O) → H^1(Y,O)) < ∞ for Y ⋐ X (hence dim H^1(X,O) = g < ∞ for compact X), with consequences: existence of meromorphic functions with a prescribed pole (Thm 14.12), meromorphic functions taking prescribed values / separating points (Cor 14.13), and vanishing results for non-compact surfaces (Thm 14.15, Cor 14.16).
- **§15 The Exact Cohomology Sequence** — book 118-126, PDF 124-132. Sheaf homomorphisms, kernels, exact sequences (15.1-15.9), the connecting homomorphism (15.11), the long exact cohomology sequence of a short exact sheaf sequence (Thm 15.12, book 123 / PDF 129), H^1(X,F) ≅ H(X)/βG(X) when H^1(X,G) = 0 (Thm 15.13), Dolbeault's Theorem H^1(X,O) ≅ E^(0,1)/d''E and H^1(X,Ω) ≅ E^(2)/d E^(1,0) (Thm 15.14, book 125 / PDF 131), and deRham's Theorem H^1(X,C) ≅ Rh^1(X) (book 126 / PDF 132).
- **§16 The Riemann–Roch Theorem** — book 126-132, PDF 132-138. Divisors, degree, the sheaves O_D (16.1-16.5), and **the Riemann–Roch Theorem: Thm 16.9 (book 129-130 / PDF 135-136), dim H^0(X,O_D) − dim H^1(X,O_D) = 1 − g + deg D**, with the index of speciality (16.10), existence of a non-constant function with pole of order ≤ g+1 (Thm 16.11), X admits a covering of P^1 with ≤ g+1 sheets (Cor 16.12), and genus 0 ⇒ X ≅ P^1 (Cor 16.13).
- **§17 The Serre Duality Theorem** — book 132-146, PDF 138-152. **17.1 (residue linear form Res: H^1(X,Ω) → C) and 17.2 (Mittag-Leffler distributions of 1-forms and their residues) at book 132-133 / PDF 138-139**, Res(μ) = Res([δμ]) (Thm 17.3), **17.4 (the sheaves Ω_D of 1-form multiples of −D, with Ω_D ≅ O_{D+K}) at book 134 / PDF 140**, the dual pairing and ι_D (17.5-17.6), and **the Serre Duality Theorem: Thm 17.9, ι_D: H^0(X,Ω_{−D}) ≅ H^1(X,O_D)* at book 138 / PDF 144**; corollaries include dim H^1(X,O_D) = dim H^0(X,Ω_{−D}) (17.10), g = dim Ω(X) (17.10), deg(ω) = 2g − 2 for a canonical divisor (Thm 17.12), tori have genus 1 (17.13), the Riemann–Hurwitz formula (17.14, book 139-140), the vanishing H^1(X,O_D) = 0 for deg D > 2g − 2 (17.16), and the projective embedding theorem.
- **§20 Abel's Theorem** — book 159-166, PDF 165-172. Weak solutions of divisors and logarithmic differentiation (20.1-20.2), the residue-type integral lemma (20.3), 1-chains, 1-cycles and homology (20.4), weak solutions along chains (Lemma 20.5), harmonic forms dual to closed curves (Cor 20.6), and **Abel's Theorem: Thm 20.7 (book 163-164 / PDF 169-170), a degree-0 divisor D is principal iff there is a 1-chain c with ∂c = D and ∫_c ω = 0 for every ω ∈ Ω(X)**.
- **§21 The Jacobi Inversion Problem** — book 166-175, PDF 172-181. Lattices in real vector spaces (21.1), period lattices Per(ω_1,...,ω_g) (21.2), **Lemma 21.3 (g points on which no nonzero holomorphic 1-form vanishes) and Thm 21.4 (the period lattice is a genuine lattice in C^g) at book 168-170 / PDF 174-176**, H_1(X) ≅ Z^{2g} (21.5), the Jacobi variety Jac(X) = C^g/Per and Picard group (21.6), and the Jacobi Inversion Theorem j: Pic_0(X) ≅ Jac(X) (Thm 21.7, book 171 / PDF 177) plus surjectivity of X^g → Jac(X) (21.8).
- **Appendix (A. Partitions of Unity; B. Topological Vector Spaces)** — book 237-242, PDF 243-248. Part A (book 237-238 / PDF 243-244) covers C^∞ bump functions, manifolds, differentiable charts/atlases (A.1-A.2) and the existence of partitions of unity subordinate to a covering; Part B (book 238-242 / PDF 244-248) collects the facts on Fréchet/topological vector spaces (L. Schwartz's theorem on compact perturbations of epimorphisms) used in the finiteness and Runge theorems.
